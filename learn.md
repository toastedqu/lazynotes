# The Journey
During my self-study journey, I frequently faced pushback from my own brain - it never felt right. I kept studying each concept/method in isolation, as if each one were just something to shovel into my head for interviews. That's low-level understanding. I tried to structure my study notes in a systemic, high-level fashion, but it never came out right.

This constant back-and-forth in my head is why the LazyNotes project has stalled and restarted for years. Funny enough, I ran into something quite similar at work very recently — an ultimate objective that keeps shifting, so whatever structure gets built on top of it doesn't hold, and no action is efficient or effective.

I entered the field not to get a job but to build a true AI like Jarvis from Iron Man, and nothing I've been doing has consistently aimed at that goal:
- I need the notes to pass interviews (they did help, sure, but I still don't understand shit.)
- I need the notes to look good so that people can benefit from them (why should I give a fuck about others when I don't even understand shit myself.)
- I do want to become an expert in ML/DL/NLP (but I keep forgetting shit - not because of a lack of practice, but because of a lack of intuitive, systemic understanding.)

So what do I actually want to achieve from all this self-study? And what makes it consistently meaningful & enjoyable?
