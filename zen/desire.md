# 3. Desire

This chapter dissects desire rationally. The nastier land - emotions - comes next.

&nbsp;

## Prerequisites

### Uncertainty & Expectation
Uncertainty threatens survival, so the brain evolved to predict. For every scenario it runs a probability estimate to shrink the unknown. Call it **expectation**.
- Something good is coming: fine.
- Something bad is coming: prepare, minimize the loss.
- Unknown: no preparation, and the loss finds us anyway.

But everything is computation, and computation means change. Every state, phenomenon, and attribute is in transition. Change is the only certainty. Everything else is uncertain by default.

Theoretically, prediction works: given ALL INFO about the current state, the next state follows.

Practically, ALL INFO is unreachable. Every phenomenon depends on every other, and we observe a fragment of the network. Missing info guarantees prediction error. That error *is* uncertainty, quantified.

So expectation can never do the job it was built for. Worse, it smuggles in a demand. The brain scores each prediction against the self's survival, then insists on the favorable branch. A guess becomes a claim on reality.

Reality doesn't give a fuck about our estimates.

**Expectation is an illusion.**

&nbsp;

### Utility & Meaning
Computation has a "how": cause + conditions → consequence. Whether it has a "why" is beyond human perception.

But we don't give a fuck about "why computation". We only give a fuck about "why us". The self cannot sit with an unanswered "why us", because a character without a plot is nobody. The model demands that the story mean something, in order to certify that **the protagonist matters**.

So we search:
- We find a meaning (e.g., a religion, a mission, a legacy, etc.). Now we must defend it, because our significance rests on a concept, and concepts are empty.
- We find none, and we call the absence "despair", as if a promised delivery failed to arrive.

Both make the same mistake: treating meaning as an attribute of reality, out there to be found or missed. Attributes live in the model. Reality is empty of them.

**Meaning is an illusion.**

&nbsp;

### Control
The self demands a favorable state. Control claims the power to produce it.

Something is controllable if we can make absolute predictions about the computations associated with it. Expectation already showed why we cannot.

Now look from another angle.

No phenomenon has a fixed, separable self-identity. Every change in it depends on countless other phenomena, most of which we cannot observe, let alone steer. Zoom out one scale, and our influence on anything rounds to negligible.

We don't even run our own machinery. The 5 aggregates and 6 sense bases keep their own schedule - thoughts arrive uninvited, perceptions shift without permission. If we cannot control "ourselves", what exactly is controlling everything else?

Read this sentence: "We influence things." There is no "we". There are no "things". There is no "influence". There is one process, computation, and it just runs. The sense of steering is a story told after the fact - a narrator taking credit for a scene it only watched.

The story survives because control feels safe, and safety scores well on perceived survival.

**Control is an illusion.**

&nbsp;

## Desire

From the previous chapter:
- Greed pulls toward states labeled "good".
- Hatred pushes against states labeled "bad".
- Ignorance is the demand that reality hold still in a favorable configuration.

Desire derives from the "self" illusion. "Self" is empty by nature, so it wants things to fill the hole.

But computation dictates everything, so the self cannot always get what it wants.

This discrepancy between demand & supply is the sole nature of all our life problems.

2 attributes decide the perceived size of the problem:
- **Return**: How much utility the self believes it gains by closing the gap. Roughly, `Return = Demand - Supply`. The bigger the hole, the more filling it promises.
- **Risk**: How unlikely the self believes that closure is.

This gives 4 scenarios:

|                 | High Return        | Low Return               |
| --------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------ |
| **Low Risk**   | "YES!" 🥳          |  "Good." 🙂  |
| **High Risk**  | "Fuck..." 😵‍💫       | "Meh..." 🙄  |

```{note} Example: Romance

1. **Offered** (low return, low risk): Someone loves you a lot, but you don't really care.

2. **Ignored** (low return, high risk): Someone doesn't care much about you, and you don't really care either.

3. **Held** (high return, low risk): Your crush loves you back, and you're obsessed with them, so you're genuinely happy.

4. **Hooked** (high return, high risk): Your crush doesn't care much about you, but you're obsessed with them, so you're genuinely fucked.
```

Life consists of all 4, BUT the self pays almost all of its attention to the bottom-left cell.

&nbsp;

### Computation & Risk-Return
Risk & return are human-perceived attributes, so they inherit the properties of computation:
- **Impermanence**: Risk & Return are always changing.
- **Unity**: Risk & Return are always dependent on each other.

For example, watch **Hooked** decay under impermanence:
- Your crush gets harder to reach over time (risk rises). You become more certain the investment cannot pay out, so perceived return drops. Eventually you move on. **Hooked → Ignored**.
- Your crush suddenly likes you back and floods you with attention (risk collapses). You're happy at first, but perceived return drops too, because they're simply always there. **Hooked → Held → Offered**.

Now watch unity, and notice the direction that's easy to miss: **Risk manufactures return.**

The distance is doing the pricing. Unavailability is read as value. Difficulty is read as worth. The self believes it has measured the object, but the object never had a number on it.

This means the self is never actually wanting the thing. It is wanting the closure of a distance, and mistaking the distance for the thing's quality.

So **Hooked** is unstable in reality & self-reinforcing in perception. Reality keeps decaying it. The model keeps rebuilding it, because every day you don't get it is another day of evidence that it must be worth getting.

And you cannot audit this from the inside. The estimate is what you're seeing with. Nobody experiences "my perceived return is inflated by risk." They experience "this one is different."

&nbsp;

## Neediness, the "sweet" spot

High risk means high uncertainty, which the self absolutely hates. High return means high utility, which the self absolutely loves.

So we get a twisted trade-off between love & hate, aimed at one object. We desperately want it, and we cannot estimate whether we'll get it.

This is the "sweet" spot.

We react emotionally. We spend enormous time thinking about it. We do everything in our power to estimate the risk and mitigate it - to "control" the scenario.

But expectation & control are illusions. You never hold enough info for an accurate prediction, and computation runs the outcome regardless. You get partial info and partial influence. That is the ceiling, and you hit it early.

Somewhere in that process, the grammar changes:

- **Desire**: I want it.
- **Neediness**: I need it.

Want is a preference. Need is a survival claim. Neediness is the moment the self stops treating the gap as one variable among many BUT INSTEAD as the variable its existence depends on.

Nothing in reality changed. The self simply rewired its survival score onto an outcome it cannot compute, and then went looking for guarantees in a universe that issues none.

Everything that follows is a downstream of neediness:

- **The expectation corrupts:** You weight evidence by hope. Ambiguity reads as encouragement. You are NOT predicting BUT negotiating with the prediction.
- **The action corrupts:** Needy action optimizes for reassurance instead of the outcome. You trade a large probability for a small certainty, over and over, and call it trying.
- **The return inflates:** Every hour spent is a cost already paid, and a cost already paid must have been worth paying. So the model raises the value to justify the bill.
- **The loop tightens:** Higher return → more attention → more investment → higher return. Positive feedback with no upper bound in the model, and a hard bound in reality.

Neediness reliably damages the thing it's trying to secure, arithmetically. You are pushing hardest on the exact computation you understand least.

&nbsp;

### (Digression) Manipulation
Manipulation is leading others to supply what you demand. By that definition, every interpersonal interaction is manipulation.

What the everyday word points at is 2 moves running at once:
1. **Swap the supply/demand roles:** Make them believe they demand what you supply instead.
2. **Hold the high-risk-high-return profile:** Keep their perceived return high and their perceived risk high, so nothing resolves, so they keep believing they NEED you.

Note the second move requires almost nothing. Intermittent availability holds risk high, and risk manufactures return. The manipulator supplies the conditions. The target's own computation does the rest.

So nobody makes anybody needy. Neediness is what the model does with the unresolved gap.

&nbsp;

## The second arrow

Ironically, wanting to kill desire is also a desire.

Peace is the high return. Your own machinery is the high risk, since you cannot control what you desire. The "sweet" spot has simply acquired a new object: "you".

"I need to stop needing!" Then,
- **The expectation corrupts:** You interpret calmness as evidence of progress and get pissed off when calmness is gone.
- **The action corrupts:** You perform detachment which ironically drags you further away from true detachment.
- **The return inflates:** The more you try to push away desire, the more you believe you are doing the right thing.
- **The loop tightens:** LOL.

&nbsp;

## Solution?

Unfortunately, a deep rational understanding of all of this can't help you do shit. (This is also the standing criticism of psychology as a subject - knowing how humans work does not make you work differently.)

The understanding & the machinery are NOT the same computation. The understanding is one state in the network. The machinery is the network. A model of the process cannot exempt itself from the process.

You never decided any of it. You did not choose the demand. You did not choose its return, its risk, or the priority it was assigned. By the time "you" arrive, the gap has already been measured and already been scored.

Computation controls everything. You don't.

Why? Because you are born with emotions.
