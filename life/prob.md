# Problem
Computation absolutely doesn't give a fuck about us.

&nbsp;

## Root
1. **Survival instinct** is the root cause of your entire life experience.
    - Evolution fostered the following core properties of humans:
        - **5 Aggregates**: Form, Feeling, Perception, Mental Formation, Consciousness.
        - **6 Sense Bases**: Eye, Ear, Nose, Tongue, Body, Mind.
    - At any given point, your brain only wants to optimize your perceived survival capabilities, even if that leads to actions against survival.
2. **Conditionality** is ingrained in your brain throughout centuries of evolution.
    - You perceive different phenomena with different dualistic labels based on how they affect your survival.
    - Hence, your experience with life becomes purely conditioned on your perception of external phenomena.
3. **Suffering** is the rejection of reality.
    - When you label life as "good", it's easy to accept reality because this label satisfies survival.
    - When you label life as "bad" due to your negative perception of external phenomena, you reject reality because this label threatens survival.
    - → You suffer not because your life is intrinsically bad, but because:
        - You perceive it as bad, which your brain interprets as "your survival is threatened."
        - You want to alter reality, so that "your survival is secured."
4. **Attachment** is the root of suffering.
    - When you perceive certain external phenomena as beneficial for your survival, you form attachment to them, meaning "you give a fuck".
    - Such attachment to "good" external phenomena has 3 forms, where the last includes the first 2:
        - **Greed**: Cling to what you want.
        - **Hatred**: Push away what you don't want.
        - **Ignorance**: Ignore reality.
    - Attachment causes suffering if:
        - You cannot get what you want.
        - You get what you don't want.
        - You lose what you love.
        - You cannot lose what you hate.
    - Attachment & Conditionality assumes control, certainty, staticity, dualism, and concept existence, all of which are illusions created and reinforced by computation, originating from survival instinct.

If you are not facing extreme threats to your physical survival, you suffer only because:
1. **You give a fuck.**
2. **You cannot see reality.**

As soon as you were born, your movie started, and you think you are the main character in your movie.

**Unfortunately, it's all just an illusion created by computation, but you get so absorbed into your own movie that you no longer see reality.**

## Ration
### Uncertainty
Uncertainty threatens survival because we don't know what's going to happen.
- If we know something good is going to happen, ok.
- If we know something bad is going to happen, we can prepare for it to minimize our loss and secure our survival.
- If we don't know what's going to happen, there's a chance our survival is negatively affected AND we cannot prepare for it.

Everything is changing - state, phenomenon, attribute. Change is the only certainty in the observable universe. Therefore, technically, everything is uncertain to us. Throughout evolution, human brains have learnt to make probability estimations (i.e., **expectation**) for various scenarios to mitigate the negative impact from uncertainty.

Theoretically, at any time point, we can ALWAYS predict the next state from the current state, as long as we have ALL the info.

Practically, it is impossible for us to obtain all the info because of unity (i.e., all phenomena are dependent on each other).

The lack of info leads to prediction errors, which quantifies uncertainty.

Hence, **expectation is an illusion**.

&nbsp;

### Uncontrollability
Something is controllable if we can make absolute predictions on the computations associated with it. We just learnt this is practically impossible.

I want to talk about control from another angle.

No phenomenon has a fixed separable self identity, so the changes associated with a phenomenon is always dependent on many other phenomena that we cannot always observe. That means, our direct influence on a phenomenon is likely negligible on a larger temporal-spatial scale.

We don't even have total control over our own 5 Aggregates & 6 Sense Bases. If we cannot even control "ourselves", how can we control external phenomena?

More importantly, we think "we" "influence" "things", but in reality it's just computation doing its own thing. There's no "we". There's no "things". There's no "influence".

Control gives us a sense of safety for survival. Unfortunately, for the sake of survival, we fail to see **control is an illusion**.

## Emotion
### Fear
Fear is two-layered:
- We perceive the target as a threat to our survival.
- We have little to no control/expectation over the target.

Basically, there is a threat, and we can do nothing about it, so we fear it. Oftentimes, you'd notice your fear is gone as soon as you find a viable option to control/predict the target.
