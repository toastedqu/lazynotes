### Loss Aversion & Attachment
There are levels to desires. "Self" typically separates desires into 2 main categories:
- **"Want"**: I am better off with them.
- **"Need"**: I am worse off without them.

"Want" is typically healthy. If we succeed, we gain. If we fail, nothing changes. It's a positive-neutral situation.

"Need" is typically unhealthy, depending on the exact target. If we succeed, we gain. If we fail, we lose. It's a positive-negative situation.

Loss aversion is, by definition, associated with "needs", not "wants". A missed gain only forgoes a chance to reproduce later, BUT a loss may drop "self" below the survival threshold. Throughout millions of years of evolution, brains that overweighted the downside left more descendants, causing loss aversion.

In fact, loss aversion is so deeply ingrained in our DNA that it has a critical impact on our perception of value. We can take one thing for granted and only truly realize our deep-rooted need for it when we lose it. Get thrown into the middle of the Sahara Desert, and now you finally recognize how valuable water was. The bestie you friendzoned for years just got a partner. Suddenly you find them more attractive. Loss is the fastest way for a person to switch from "I don't give a fuck" to "I give a fuck".

This is a critical distinction. We only give a fuck about things we give a fuck about. We don't give a fuck about things we don't give a fuck about. How does the "self" distinguish the two? Of course, it varies by person, but humans share a common pool of fundamental needs - food, water, sleep, fitness, etc. We give a fuck about the things we perceive as "needs". Perceived survival is threatened if the needs are not satisfied, so the "self" constantly seeks methods to satisfy the needs, till they are satisfied. 

This is **attachment**. We pay much more attention to these needs. Fear of loss comes into play. ANY subtle signal from the needs gets amplified, processed, and analyzed in depth. As these needs remain unsatisfied, we gradually form stronger and stronger emotional attachment to them, causing obsession and ultimately giving up control of our emotions to them.

### Expectation & Control
To optimize its objectives, "self" needs to first know its current level with respect to the objective. 

The brain evolved to run probability estimation (i.e., **expectation**) in various scenarios for calibration, summarizable in 3 situations:
- **"Good" is coming**: Ok.
- **"Bad" is coming**: Prepare to minimize the loss.
- **Unknown**: Hard to prepare. Cannot minimize the loss, if any.

Computation means change, so everything changes, so everything is uncertain by default regardless of how well the brain estimates the probabilities.

Theoretically, expectation works - given ALL INFO about the current state, the next state follows.

Practically, ALL INFO is unreachable. We can only observe a fragment of the computation network.

Missing info guarantees prediction error, which we call **uncertainty** - the inevitable major threat to the optimization process.

**Control** is the illusory ability to alter the direction & magnitude of computation. Control gives "self" the feeling that it can minimize uncertainty & achieve its objectives with certainty.

But control is BS. Computation dictates everything. Anything "external" is beyond the control of "self". What "self" consistently fails to realize is - anything "internal" is ALSO beyond the control of "self". The 5 aggregates & 6 senses are beyond the control of "self". Computation has dictated them since the birth of "self".

If we stay in the illusory realm, the only thing "self" effectively has control over is its own action given the current state.
