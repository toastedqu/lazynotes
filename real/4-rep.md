Repetition. 

Nothing more than that. I know it's the most boring answer that computation has already tricked your brain into objecting to, but that is the ONLY true answer that works in reality.

Computation is the true reality. Computation created & manages this illusory survival game. The rules are all written by computation.

## 4.1 Action

Recall that computation is state transition. Given a cause and a set of conditions, a consequence is guaranteed to take place upon computation. That's the fundamental mechanism of computation, and therefore,

**You reap what you sow.**

This is a cliché, but this is the absolute, fundamental mechanism of computation. Actions have consequences. Your actions have consequences on you. If you go to the gym, your body becomes more fit. If you study more effective workout routines, your body becomes more fit at a faster rate. If you listen to a lecture with full attention, your brain learns the knowledge. If you eat potato chips late at night, you gain more fat. If you reject someone whom you truly value as a friend but nothing more, you feel guilt and grief. If you face a rejection and stay composed, your frame becomes stronger. If you notice your anger without being absorbed into it, your emotional awareness becomes sharper. If you face your deep-rooted insecurity fully and continue moving, you become emotionally stronger. If you drown yourself in alcohol to alleviate the pain you are feeling, you become emotionally weaker.

The causal relationship is absolute. "Right" actions cause "right" consequences. "Wrong" actions cause "wrong" consequences.

**You absolutely reap what you sow.**

## 4.2 Inertia

We've been framing everything as a "you vs computation" or "internal vs external" thing, but don't forget - computation is the true reality. We are just slaves to computation. We are illusions. We don't exist. There is only computation. I did the separation just for explanation purposes.

Why do I bring this up right here, right now? Because a critical, often overlooked, human-specific rule of computation is that every action we take has a consequence on our brain. AI professionals know exactly what I'm talking about, and it's true. When we take an action, there are countless ways in which our brain network gets rewired, but one major process is:
1. Our current neural network makes a decision to take an action, which results in a consequence.
2. We receive & interpret a signal from the consequence.
3. We associate our taken action with the signal & adjust our neural network to fit this association.
4. When facing a similar scenario in the future, this new neural network will make a new decision dependent on this association.

Looks harmless, right? By the way, this is how humans get easily manipulated & exploited in all scenarios.

Potato chips make you fat. Why can't you stop eating them? Your brain has learnt that eating them makes you feel "better". It associates potato chips with rewards. Why can others resist the temptation? Their brain has learnt that eating them makes them feel "worse". It associates potato chips with penalties.

*But I don't want this association! When did my brain learn this?*

Every, single, moment.

Every single action you took has rewired your brain, so every time you face a similar situation, your brain tends to repeat the learned action by default based on what it has already learnt in the past.

*Wait... But I thought I had control over my intentional actions?*

Yeah, you still do. Where did you see "intention" in anything I've said so far? Did you intentionally rewire your brain based on every action you took in the past? NO! This inertia in your brain is a product of dependent origination (i.e., none other than computation)!

**You are absolutely the sum of your actions.**

Not because you intentionally choose to be the sum of your actions, but because computation dictates the rules. Every single action you took rewired your brain accordingly, including unintentional actions, impulsive reactions, etc., basically dependent originations for the most part.

That's why you procrastinate. That's why you indulge in instant gratification. That's why you stay in your comfort zone. That's why you have certain mental issues or personality defects or certain tendencies you weren't born with. That's why you avoid pain. Not any BS rational reason circulating out there. No. It's all just fucking computation.

## 4.3 Intention & Repetition

*So we are fucked.*

Yeah. You ever heard of the clichéd self-help advice **"take small steps, one at a time"**? Yeah, that's actually the only fucking solution we have. There's no shortcut. Anyone who tells you otherwise is bullshitting you. You have to consistently take tiny actions your current brain network is able to tolerate, in order to gradually rewire your brain network and eventually reach your desired state.

Not big actions. Big actions result in penalty signals like discomfort and pain, instead of reward signals. Even if one day you feel incredibly motivated, take big actions, and feel rewarded, your baseline is still there. When your motivation dies, your baseline will still take over.

That's why you have to work WITH your baseline brain, NOT AGAINST it. You have to take actions that are under the threshold of discomfort for your brain, so that your brain does not perceive the signals from your actions as penalty.

The fact that you are finding this annoying is also a consequence of computation.

What is "intention" in "intentional action" anyway? Intention is formed by consciousness, i.e., the "self", which is itself a byproduct of computation. Here, it's the "self" alerting you that you are deviating from your survival objectives. It's System 2 noticing that System 1 has messed things up a bit too much. That's why you suddenly have the motivation to "make your life better".

Every fuck-up triggers intention. Every small step makes it stick.

**You can only truly absorb life lessons by repeatedly fucking up.**

Keep in mind, computation is never your enemy. True, you have to play by the rules of computation because that's reality, but computation is **non-dualistic** by nature. YOU are the one who assigns dualistic labels to things in order to make sense of them. If you treat it as an enemy, then you will feel opposed. If you treat it as a friend, then you will feel supported.
