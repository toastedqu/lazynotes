# 3. Emotions

Depressing, ain't it? Notice how you feel "depressed". That's your body's response to all these illusions and reality.

Or maybe you feel something else. Fear. Boredom. Confusion. Maybe even happiness, depending on how you interpret it.

Your brain just perceived something, interpreted it in some way, and generated some chemicals which your body can feel. This feeling will trigger some new actions as a response to the external environment.

This, is your entire life experience.

**You experience life through emotions.**

&nbsp;

## 3.1 Parent-Child Relationship

There are 2 cognitive systems in the human brain:
- System 1: the fast, intuitive, unconcious, and emotional.
- System 2: the slow, deliberate, conscious, and rational.

System 1 is like a child. They want whatever they perceive valuable and cry when they cannot get it. They are purely emotional. Their actions are impulse-driven. They don't care about the consequences because they are incapable of thinking.

System 2, **it is your job as the rational parent to guide the child**.

Bad parenting means you get reactive toward the child. You become emotional like the child. You try your very best to control the child. You want the child to shut the fuck up. You give up, surrender to the child, and just let they do whatever they want. Annoying. Super annoying. You know. You've become the child.

Good parenting means you embrace your child fully without getting reactive. You stay calm. You listen to the child and understand what exactly they are dealing with. You tell the child what's the best thing to do and explain why in detail. The child may react even harder, and it is your job to stay rock solid.

Your ex whom you imagined a whole future with dumped you out of the blue. You feel surprised, confused, hurt, sad, devastated, angry, frustrated. You act upon these emotions - asking for an explanation, seeking resolution, arguing, throwing a tantrum, begging them to stay, crying in your bedroom alone, blabla.

That's the child.

Your parent whom showered you with nothing but love and kindness throughout your entire life died out of the blue. You feel grief, loss, fear, emptiness, sadness of course, devastation. You act upon these emotions - oh I cannot even imagine...

That's the child.

You poured your heart and soul into your life goal for years and it failed. You feel disappointment, regret, shame, guilt. You drown yourself in alcohol. You curse at everyone who tries to comfort you or reason with you. Oh "reason"? You hate this word specifically right now. You feel like a total loser and want to end it all.

That's the child.

Is it bad to be a child? Absolutely not. Computation has no dualistic label, remember?

It's not only a part of who you are in the movie of life. It's quite literally how you experience life.

People at their lowest moments want to kill the inner child and become emotionless. Why wouldn't they? It's painful to feel so much. It's a living hell. It's suffering.

But if you kill the inner child and become truly emotionless, you experience no joy either. You experience nothing. You are not "alive".

You experience life through this inner child. So you need this inner child to be there, alive and sound.

The key is to **face your emotions directly without losing your frame**.

## 3.2 The Holy Trinity vs Computation

The key contains 3 parts:
- **Emotional Strength**: "directly"
- **Emotional Awareness**: "face your emotions"
- **Frame Control**: "without losing your frame"

Fuck all the self-help BS or philosophy or psychology out there. You obtain the key, your life experience becomes optimal. All 3 parts are required.

But I want to discuss the 3 parts backwards, as it gets progressively harder to master.

&nbsp;

### Frame Control

Frame is your action plan, your policy model in reinforcement learning, your strategy for dealing with various life situations.

Quote from Chapter 1:

> In theory, there can be 2 types of computation:
> - **Dependent Origination**: The natural, universal computation.
> - **Intentional Action**: The computation invoked by a mind-forming intention (i.e., consciousness).
> 
> "I die from an illness" is a dependent origination. "I die from jumping off a building" is an intentional action.
> 
> In reality, everything is dependent origination. We differentiate for the sake of explanations.

Yeah, in reality, there's just computation, but thankfully we live our life in the illusory realm created by computation, so "intentional action" is still here, and **you should remember the distinction between intentional action and dependent origination for the rest of your life.**

Frame is our internal guide for "intentional action". Frame is the ONLY thing we have control over in the survival game created by computation.

I strongly disagree with all the self-help productivity BS out there. Such idiotic constraints to limit your life experience. There is NO TRUE frame you should follow. No. None. Computation is the true reality anyway. 

You define your own frame. You want to work? Work. You want to sleep? Sleep. You want to eat? Eat. You want to get drunk? Get drunk. The key point here is NOT what you do, but that you do it **with full commitment**.

You define your frame, and you stick to it regardless of whatever comes your way. Remember,

**Frame is yours. Everything else is computation.**

A strong, clear frame is the compass for you to navigate life amid chaos. You can revise your frame however you like, whenever and wherever you want, BUT don't lose it. When you take an action that you did NOT intend, you lose your frame.

*So why is frame control overpowered?*

Say you want to get a job at a top company. You've designed your policy - daily applications, resume polishing, networking, interview preps, etc. Your dream companies have rejected you repeatedly. 

If you are bad at frame control, these rejections can easily crush your frame, making you doubt yourself, question your capabilities, and lose motivation. You stop taking intentional actions and start letting computation control your actions. You lose your frame and lose your chance of landing a dream job.

If you are good at frame control, your frame is independent of ANYTHING, so these rejections don't change your policy whatsoever. You continue taking intentional actions, and your odds of landing a dream job remains. Are you guaranteed to get it? No, but are your odds lowered? Also no. In fact, they are likely improved because of your strong, persistent frame.

It's all about continuing intentional actions instead of letting dependent origination take over. As aforementioned, the distinction between intention and computation is crucial.

**You need absolute certainty in your frame to live in accordance with uncertainty.**

### Emotional Awareness

Emotional Awareness is noticing & facing your emotions. As we only experience life through emotions and everything else is just an illusion created by computation, I disagree with the conventional definition and advocation of awareness. Thoughts are also products of computation. We can have conscious thoughts, which belong to intentional action. We can also have unconscious thoughts (which happen significantly more often), which belong to dependent origination. Being aware of thoughts is ok, but it's also a trap because you tend to easily get absorbed into those thoughts by adding more thoughts on top of them. Once you start thinking about thoughts at all, you easily get into an endless spiral of thoughts.

Conscious thoughts are already a part of our frame. Any intentional action is a part of our frame. Unconscious thoughts are not, so they inflict emotions. And the emotions are the parts we truly need to be aware of. Again, they are NOT the enemy. They are the experience. They are the child. So, you notice them, and you look at them.

*So why is emotional awareness overpowered?*

Say you asked your crush out and they laughed at your face and shamed you in front of your shared social circle. Because of the existence of System 1, you will very likely have impulsive thoughts and intense emotions.

Now, if you engage with these impulsive thoughts, you will hear some bullshits about self-protection and self-inadequacy, like "Why did they go that far? That's so unnecessary!" or "I must protect myself or I will be kicked out of the group and I will no longer be safe." or "Bitch go fuck yourself who the fuck do you think you are?!" blabla bullshits. If you engage with these thoughts, they will take over your actions, and you lose your frame. We can only imagine what happens afterwards... All according to computation.

If you engage with the intense emotions instead, you will notice something very interesting - You see anger. You see frustration. You see immediate sadness. You see self-blame. You see self-doubt. You see fear. You see all of them. And you will form thoughts like "I feel angry" or "I feel mad" or "I feel scared" and so on.

What's beautiful here is - you are not really in the story written by external computation. You are in your own story with your child. It's almost as if, the story computation just wrote doesn't really matter. Only your story matters at the moment - **You feel hurt**. And that's it. That's your reality (and yep, that actually is your current reality because that's the ongoing computation right here right now).

**You suffer because you aren't here right now.**

But that's barely enough.

### Emotional Strength

Emotional strength is the single most overpowered skill that everyone should absolutely master. It is IMHO so much more important than the 2 skills above. You can have no frame at all, no action plan for anything. You can get yourself swamped in endless thoughts and emotions. But if you have emotional strength, you can STILL take intentional actions amid ANYTHING. And that's all that matters - internal computation vs external computation.

*So how? And what exactly is emotional strength?* I only defined it with one word - "directly". Why? Let's follow the previous example.

> You feel hurt.

Pain is a direct signal from your brain that your perceived survival is at stake. Every single negative emotion originates from pain. Every single negative scenario results in pain. Pain is specifically designed by computation to feel super terrible and hard to ignore, in order to alert you to take immediate actions - to ensure your perceived survival. 

You see the problem? As soon as computation presses the "Pain" button, it can easily take over your body, your mind, your attention, your frame, everything of you. Why? Because your perceived survival is threatened, and you are designed to immediately do something about it. It's literally the underlying biological mechanism.

Strength is, by definition, **"the ability to resist being moved or broken by a force"** ([Merriam-Webster Dictionary](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/strength)).

Emotional strength is, then, the abililty to resist being moved or broken by emotions. Since positive emotions are typically not going to break us (but not always), the main challenge is with negative emotions. Since all negative emotions originates from pain, emotional strength is the ability to resist being moved or broken by pain. And what's the underlying force behind pain (and any emotion at all)? Computation.

Emotional strength is, by my own definition, **the ability to resist being moved or broken by computation**.

Now you see why it's much more important than frame control and emotional awareness, both of which are supplementary to emotional strength.

*But, didn't you just say in the last chapter that we are slaves to computation?*

Yes, and we always are, but we don't have to yield. Remember Sisyphus? He kept pushing the boulder, even though it was futile. He made sense of the struggle, even though it was inevitable. **THAT IS US WITH COMPUTATION!**

Look how absurd our miserable life is. We are born to die. Yet we are somehow given some bullshit objectives to optimize for in this illusory realm created by computation. We are pushing the boulder till the day we die.

If you can face all emotions, see reality, accept the absurdity of computation, and take intentional actions anyway, that is true strength.

That, is how overpowered emotional strength is. It bypasses ALL filters & directly confronts reality - computation itself.

It won't make life easier. Nothing will. But it will make you unbreakable & uncontrollable by computation.

Whatever situation computation puts you in, you face it fully and take intentional actions anyway. You don't avoid it. You don't waste time or energy or attention on engaging with it. You just act.

And in doing so, you become stronger than computation itself.
